Module 01: Setup

Lab Rules & Online Classroom Protocols

To simulate the high-intensity, structured environment of a Science Olympiad tournament, we follow strict lab codes during our online sessions:

01

Live Cameras: Stay Present

Keep your video feeds active. Science Olympiad partners must work together seamlessly, and face-to-face feedback helps us debug circuits faster.

02

Muted by Default

Prevent ambient feedback. Press the spacebar or use zoom controls to speak. Use the "Raise Hand" function for questions during live lectures.

03

Interactive Response

We use live polls and competitive quizzes (like Kahoot!). All students are expected to answer every check-for-understanding prompt.

The Safe Zone: Fail Forward

In electrical engineering, "magic smoke" or broken pathways are valuable data. Do not be afraid to answer incorrectly. Every mistake is a learning data point.

🔋 Edison's Direct Current (DC)

Current flows constantly in one direction. Safe at low voltages, but cannot travel long distances without massive power loss.

⚡ Tesla's Alternating Current (AC)

Current periodically reverses direction. Easy to scale up or down using transformers, making it perfect for long-distance power lines.

Module 04: Configurations

Pathways: Series vs. Parallel Circuits

Series Circuit Simulator

Series Pathway

All components are chained in a single continuous path. If any single part is broken or open, the entire path halts and all currents drop to zero immediately!

  • Current (I) remains identical through all components.
  • Total Voltage divides across each bulb proportional to resistance.
  • Equivalent Resistance: $R_{eq} = R_1 + R_2$.

💡

Olympiad Quick Math Trap

In parallel, equivalent resistance is ALWAYS smaller than the smallest individual resistor. If you calculate $R_{eq}$ for a parallel loop and it is larger than a single branch, recalculate!

Module 05: Practical Lab

Component Lab & Cheat Sheet Tips

Resistor (Fixed)

Basic Passives

Schematic Symbol

Functional Description

Opposes electric current to limit flow rate. Protects fragile components (like LEDs) from receiving too much current. Uses color bands to denote value and tolerance rating.

⚠️

SO Trap: Color Band Reading

The standard four-band resistor will have multiplier bands that can easily be misread if you look from the wrong end. Always locate the Gold/Silver tolerance band first; read from the opposite end!

Module 06: Quiz Time

Division B Circuit Lab Warm-up

Question 1 of 5 Score: 0 pts
If a circuit is powered by a 9V battery and has an equivalent resistance of 150 ohms, what is the total current running through the circuit in milliamperes (mA)?

Explanation

Ohm's Law: I = V / R = 9 / 150 = 0.06 A. Converting to milliamperes: 0.06 A * 1000 = 60 mA.
