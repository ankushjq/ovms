Part 1: Capacitance

What is a Capacitor? (Electrostatic Storage)

Core Physics & Structure

A capacitor stores electrical energy in an electrostatic field. It consists of two conducting metal plates separated by an insulating gap called the dielectric (such as air, paper, or ceramic).

Unlike batteries, which release energy slowly via chemical reactions, capacitors charge and discharge almost instantaneously.

Formula: C = Q / V
C = Capacitance (Farads, F)
Q = Accumulated Charge (Coulombs, C)
V = Voltage Difference (Volts, V)

Capacitor Units & Conventions

The unit of capacitance is the Farad (F), named after Michael Faraday.

A 1 Farad capacitor is massive! In standard electronic circuits, we use much smaller sub-units. Middle schoolers must master metric unit conversions:

  • Microfarad ($\mu\text{F}$): $10^{-6}\text{F}$ (one-millionth of a Farad)
  • Nanofarad ($\text{nF}$): $10^{-9}\text{F}$ (one-billionth of a Farad)
  • Picofarad ($\text{pF}$): $10^{-12}\text{F}$ (one-trillionth of a Farad)
Test Trap: Many Science Olympiad exam problems list capacitance in microfarads, but you must convert them to base Farads before solving equations!

Capacitor Configurations: Series vs. Parallel

Parallel Pathway Configuration

When capacitors are connected in parallel, the battery connects to all of them directly. This effectively combines their plate surface areas together.

C_eq = C1 + C2 + C3 ...
Example Calculation:
Find equivalent capacitance for a $100\,\mu\text{F}$ and a $220\,\mu\text{F}$ capacitor in parallel:
$C_{eq} = 100\,\mu\text{F} + 220\,\mu\text{F} = 320\,\mu\text{F}$

Series Pathway Configuration

When capacitors are connected in series, they are placed in a single line. This increases the effective dielectric gap thickness, reducing overall capacitance.

1 / C_eq = 1 / C1 + 1 / C2 + 1 / C3 ...
Example Calculation:
Find equivalent capacitance for two $10\,\mu\text{F}$ capacitors in series:
$1/C_{eq} = 1/10 + 1/10 = 2/10 = 1/5 \implies C_{eq} = 5\,\mu\text{F}$

RC Timing & Charging Curves

The RC Time Constant ($\tau$)

When a resistor ($R$) is connected in series with a charging capacitor ($C$), the resistor limits the flow rate of charge (current). This creates a predictable timing curve.

We define the rate of charging using the Time Constant ($\tau$, Tau):

τ = R × C

The 5τ Rule: It takes exactly $1\tau$ (one time constant) for a capacitor to charge up to 63.2% of its maximum voltage. It is considered fully charged (99.3%) after $5\tau$.

Water Tank Analogy 🚰

Think of a capacitor like an empty water tank, the battery as a water pump, and the resistor as a narrow pipe:

  • The water tank's floor area is Capacitance (C). Larger area holds more water.
  • The narrow pipe is Resistance (R). Narrower pipes restrict the flow of water.
  • When you turn the pump on, water rushes in quickly because the tank is empty. As the tank fills, the back pressure slows water down until it stops entirely.
Test Trap: Microfarads ($\mu\text{F} = 10^{-6}\text{F}$), nanofarads ($\text{nF} = 10^{-9}\text{F}$), and picofarads ($\text{pF} = 10^{-12}\text{F}$) must always be converted to basic Farads before calculating $\tau = R \times C$!

Semiconductors: Diodes & LED Safety

Diode Behavior & Polarity

A diode acts as a one-way electrical check valve, allowing current to flow in one direction but blocking it in reverse.

  • Forward Biased (ON): Anode (+) connected to higher voltage, Cathode (-) connected to lower voltage. Current flows.
  • Reverse Biased (OFF): Polarity flipped. The diode acts like an open switch, blocking current.

LEDs (Light Emitting Diodes) are special diodes that emit light when forward current passes through them.

Key Landmark: LEDs have a longer leg (Anode +) and a shorter leg near a flat edge on the plastic body (Cathode -).

The Current-Limiting Resistor

Unlike resistors, which obey Ohm's Law linearly, diodes have a threshold called the Forward Voltage Drop ($V_f$). For typical red/green LEDs, $V_f$ is around 2.0 Volts.

Once voltage exceeds $V_f$, diode resistance drops to near zero! Without a resistor in series to limit current, current skyrockets instantly, burning the LED out.

LED Resistor Formula:
R = (V_source - V_f) / I_target
Example (9V supply, 2V LED drop, 20mA limit):
R = (9V - 2V) / 0.02A = 7V / 0.02A = 350 Ω

How a Solderless Breadboard Works

Internal Grid Architecture

Breadboards allow us to build temporary prototype circuits by plugging components directly into terminal holes. Inside, metal clips connect groups of holes together:

  • Power Rails (horizontal rows): Marked with red (+) and blue (-) lines running horizontally across the top and bottom. Used to distribute power and ground throughout the board.
  • Terminal Columns (vertical strips): Grouped into columns labeled 'a-e' and 'f-j'. Holes in column rows 1-5 (e.g. 1a, 1b, 1c, 1d, 1e) are all shorted together horizontally inside, but separated across the center divider trench.

Common Breadboarding Pitfalls

Science Olympiad build stations often check for layout errors:

  • The Trench Split: The center dividing groove separates the 'a-e' and 'f-j' columns. This is where integrated circuits (ICs) are placed so their opposing pins don't short.
  • Shorted Legs: Placing both pins of a resistor or LED in the exact same row (e.g., 5a and 5b) bypasses the component entirely, making a short circuit!
Key Practice Rule: Always keep your circuit clean. Minimize overlapping wires and cut resistor legs short to prevent accidental contacts.
