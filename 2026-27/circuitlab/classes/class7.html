1. Series & Parallel Resistance Foundations

📏 Series Resistance

Current must flow through every single resistor one after another in a single path.

Series Formula
$$R_{\text{eq}} = R_1 + R_2 + R_3 + \dots + R_n$$
✔
Total resistance is always larger than any individual resistor in series.

🔀 Parallel Resistance

Current splits across multiple parallel branches. Total resistance decreases.

Parallel Formulas & Shortcuts
• Standard: $\frac{1}{R_{\text{eq}}} = \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2} + \dots$
• 2-Resistor Shortcut: $R_{\text{eq}} = \frac{R_1 \times R_2}{R_1 + R_2}$
• $N$ Equal Resistors: $R_{\text{eq}} = \frac{R}{N}$
⚠️
Total parallel resistance is always smaller than the smallest individual branch resistor!
Segment 1 • Worked Example

Illustrated Problem #1: Combination Resistance

Problem #1 Equivalent Resistance of a Combination Loop

Find the total equivalent resistance ($R_{\text{eq}}$) of the circuit and calculate the total battery current ($I_{\text{total}}$) supplied by the 24V source.

✔ Step-by-Step Solution Derivation
Step 1: Simplify Parallel Block ($R_2 \parallel R_3$)
Using the 2-resistor shortcut: $R_{p} = \frac{20 \times 30}{20 + 30} = \frac{600}{50} = 12\,\Omega$.
Step 2: Add Series Resistor ($R_1$)
$R_{\text{total}} = R_1 + R_p = 12\,\Omega + 12\,\Omega = \mathbf{24\,\Omega}$.
Step 3: Calculate Total Battery Current ($I$)
$I_{\text{total}} = \frac{V}{R_{\text{total}}} = \frac{24\,\text{V}}{24\,\Omega} = \mathbf{1.0\,\text{Ampere}}$.

2. Series & Parallel Capacitance Foundations

⚡ Electrostatic Storage Rule

Capacitors store charge ($Q$) in Coulombs: $Q = C \times V$.

Parallel Capacitance (Area Adds!)
$$C_{\text{eq}} = C_1 + C_2 + C_3 + \dots$$
✔
Parallel capacitors ADD directly because conductive plate surface area increases!

⛓️ Series Capacitance

Series placement increases effective dielectric plate separation distance ($d$).

Series Capacitance Formula
• Standard: $\frac{1}{C_{\text{eq}}} = \frac{1}{C_1} + \frac{1}{C_2} + \dots$
• 2-Capacitor Shortcut: $C_{\text{eq}} = \frac{C_1 \times C_2}{C_1 + C_2}$
• $N$ Equal Capacitors: $C_{\text{eq}} = \frac{C}{N}$
🧠
Memory Trick: Capacitors follow the EXACT OPPOSITE formula math of Resistors!
Segment 2 • Worked Example

Illustrated Problem #2: Capacitor Network & Stored Charge

Problem #2 Capacitance Simplification & Stored Charge

Calculate total equivalent capacitance ($C_{\text{eq}}$) of the network and find total stored charge ($Q_{\text{total}}$) when connected to a 12V DC battery.

✔ Step-by-Step Solution Derivation
Step 1: Simplify Parallel Pair ($C_1 \parallel C_2$)
Parallel capacitors add directly: $C_p = C_1 + C_2 = 30\,\mu\text{F} + 30\,\mu\text{F} = 60\,\mu\text{F}$.
Step 2: Combine in Series with $C_3$
$C_{\text{eq}} = \frac{C_p \times C_3}{C_p + C_3} = \frac{60 \times 30}{60 + 30} = \frac{1800}{90} = \mathbf{20\,\mu\text{F}}$.
Step 3: Stored Charge ($Q = C \times V$)
$Q_{\text{total}} = 20 \times 10^{-6}\,\text{F} \times 12\,\text{V} = 240 \times 10^{-6}\,\text{C} = \mathbf{240\,\mu\text{C}}$ (or $0.24\,\text{mC}$).

3. Resistive Circuit Power Equations

🔥 The 3 Power Formulas

Primary Power Equation
$$P = V \times I$$
Ohm's Law Substitutions
$$P = I^2 \times R \quad \text{and} \quad P = \frac{V^2}{R}$$

Units: Power is measured in Watts ($\text{W}$) or Joules per second ($\text{J/s}$).

⚠️ Resistor Wattage & Safety

🌡️ Heat Dissipation

All electrical power consumed by a resistor is converted directly into heat. If $P_{\text{actual}} > P_{\text{rating}}$, the resistor burns out!

🚨
The Milliamp-Squared Trap: Always convert $I$ from $\text{mA}$ to $\text{A}$ BEFORE squaring! $50\,\text{mA} = 0.05\,\text{A} \implies I^2 = 0.0025\,\text{A}^2$.
⚡
Energy Formula: $E = P \times t$ (Joules or Watt-hours).
Segment 3 • Worked Example

Illustrated Problem #3: Power Dissipation & Thermal Safety

Problem #3 Resistor Power Safety Check

A student connects a resistor across a 9V DC battery. The resistor is rated for a maximum power dissipation of $1/4\text{ Watt}$ ($0.25\,\text{W}$).
Calculate the minimum safe resistance ($R_{\text{min}}$) required to prevent thermal blowout, and find the current ($I$) flowing at this limit.

✔ Step-by-Step Solution Derivation
Step 1: Choose Formula Connecting $P$, $V$, and $R$
Use $P = \frac{V^2}{R} \implies R = \frac{V^2}{P}$.
Step 2: Plug in Given Values ($V = 9\,\text{V}$, $P_{\text{max}} = 0.25\,\text{W}$)
$R_{\text{min}} = \frac{9^2}{0.25} = \frac{81}{0.25} = \mathbf{324\,\Omega}$.
Step 3: Calculate Safe Operating Current ($I$)
$I = \frac{V}{R} = \frac{9\,\text{V}}{324\,\Omega} \approx 0.0278\,\text{A} = \mathbf{27.8\,\text{mA}}$.

4. Kirchhoff's Current Law (KCL)

🌊 KCL Junction Rule

Mathematical Statement
$$\sum I_{\text{entering}} = \sum I_{\text{leaving}}$$

⚡ Physical Foundation

Conservation of Charge: Electrons cannot be created or destroyed at a node junction.

💡
At Node A: $5\,\text{A} + 3\,\text{A} = I_3 \implies \mathbf{I_3 = 8\,\text{A}}$.
Segment 4 • 35:00 - 50:00

4. Kirchhoff's Voltage Law (KVL)

🎢 KVL Loop Rule

Mathematical Statement
$$\sum V_{\text{closed loop}} = 0$$

⚡ Physical Foundation

Conservation of Energy: Total energy gained from voltage sources equals total energy lost across load resistors around a closed loop.

🧭 Sign Conventions

  • 🔋 Battery Source: Traversing from $-$ to $+$ terminal = +$V_{\text{source}}$ (Gain)
  • ⚡ Resistor: Traversing in direction of current = -$I \times R$ (Drop)
⚠️
KVL Equation: $V_{\text{source}} - I R_1 - I R_2 = 0 \implies V_{\text{source}} = I(R_1 + R_2)$.
Segment 4 • Worked Example

Illustrated Problem #4: 2-Loop KCL/KVL Circuit

Problem #4 2-Loop KCL/KVL Branch Current Solving

Use KVL to find branch currents $I_1$ and $I_2$, then use KCL at Node A to calculate total source current $I_{\text{total}}$.

✔ Step-by-Step Solution Derivation
Step 1: KVL on Loop 1 ($12\,\text{V}$ & $R_1$)
$12 - I_1(6) = 0 \implies I_1 = \frac{12}{6} = \mathbf{2.0\,\text{Amperes}}$.
Step 2: KVL on Loop 2 ($12\,\text{V}$ & $R_2$)
$12 - I_2(12) = 0 \implies I_2 = \frac{12}{12} = \mathbf{1.0\,\text{Ampere}}$.
Step 3: KCL at Node A ($I_{\text{total}} = I_1 + I_2$)
$I_{\text{total}} = 2.0\,\text{A} + 1.0\,\text{A} = \mathbf{3.0\,\text{Amperes}}$.

5. Competition Illustrated Practice Station

Challenge #1

Mixed R & C Network

Two 40µF capacitors are in parallel, connected in series with a 20µF capacitor across 10V.

Parallel: $40+40=80\,\mu\text{F}$
Series: $\frac{80 \times 20}{80 + 20} = \mathbf{16\,\mu\text{F}}$
Charge: $16\,\mu\text{F} \times 10\,\text{V} = \mathbf{160\,\mu\text{C}}$
Challenge #2

Power Ratio Comparison

Resistor A (10Ω) and Resistor B (20Ω) are connected in PARALLEL. Which dissipates more power?

In parallel, $V$ is equal.
$P = \frac{V^2}{R} \implies$ Smaller $R$ dissipates MORE power!
Resistor A dissipates 2x more power.
Challenge #3

KCL Mystery Branch

Node X has 12A entering from source. Branch 1 takes 4A, Branch 2 takes 5A. Find Branch 3 current.

$\sum I_{\text{in}} = \sum I_{\text{out}}$
$12\,\text{A} = 4\,\text{A} + 5\,\text{A} + I_3$
$\mathbf{I_3 = 3.0\,\text{Amperes}}$.
